## 1. The Ticker & Business Overview

**Innovative Solutions & Support, Inc. — now operating as "Innovative Aerosystems" (NASDAQ: IA)**

- **What they do:** Design and manufacture **certified cockpit avionics** — flat-panel glass-cockpit displays, air-data systems, flight management systems, autothrottle/autopilot, engine displays, standby instruments, and nav/com/transponder units — for **retrofit** of existing aircraft and for OEMs, across **commercial air transport, business aviation, and military** platforms. Founded 1988 by **Geoffrey S. M. Hedrick**; HQ in Exton, PA.
- **Why Wall Street ignores them:**
  - **Market cap only ~$331M** — too small for most institutions.
  - **Zero sell-side analyst coverage** — the ratings feed returned *no* analysts. This is a genuinely uncovered stock.
  - **147 employees.** A rounding error to the aerospace primes.
  - No flashy "AI company" story — it's a boring, certified-hardware business in an unglamorous niche.
  - The stock is **down ~40% from its March-2026 high of $30.94** (currently ~$18.5; 52-wk low $8.13), so it has fallen off the momentum crowd's radar.

## 3. The Competitive Moat

- **Certification is the wall.** Avionics require FAA/EASA type certificates and Supplemental Type Certificates (STCs) — years and millions of dollars per platform. IA already holds a broad library of them. A larger competitor can't simply undercut a certified, flying product.
- **Installed-base lock-in.** Its systems (and the acquired Honeywell lines) are on **thousands of aircraft**. Avionics carry 20–30 year service lives, generating sticky, high-margin **aftermarket, spares, and upgrade** revenue with near-zero customer churn — switching avionics mid-life is prohibitively expensive for an operator.
- **The giants are handing IA its niche.** Honeywell, Collins, and Garmin are **divesting** legacy autopilot/display/nav-com product lines that are too small to matter to a $100B+ prime but are franchise-makers for a $330M company. IA is the natural, low-cost consolidator of this "orphaned" avionics IP.
- **Tech/automation edge (criterion #3):** IA doesn't *build* AI — it **uses lean, software-defined, highly automated engineering and manufacturing** to fold acquired legacy product lines onto its own common integrated-cockpit platform at a **fraction of the OEM's cost structure.** That ~$570K/employee productivity is the moat in numbers: it can profitably run product lines that were marginal inside a bloated prime, expanding margins as it integrates.

## 4. The Specific Growth Trigger

**A fresh, not-yet-integrated Honeywell acquisition landing right into the next earnings report.**

- On **April 2, 2026**, IA (as Innovative Aerosystems) **acquired Honeywell's autopilot, nav/com, display, and transponder product lines** — its second major Honeywell deal (the first, June 2023, was guided to lift annualized revenue ~40% once integrated and drove FY25's near-doubling).
- This new tranche is **early in its integration ramp**, so its full revenue-synergy contribution has **not yet shown up** in reported results.
- **The catalyst:** IA's **fiscal year ends September 30, 2026** and it reports **full-year FY26 results in mid-December 2026** (FY25 was reported Dec 18, 2025). That report will be the **first clean look at the combined portfolio's run-rate** — the moment the market can quantify the new Honeywell lines plus continued military-program momentum (Q4 FY25 revenue was already +44.6% YoY, gross profit +64.8%).
- **Secondary catalysts:** continued military/retrofit program wins; the possibility of the first analyst initiating coverage (which alone often re-rates an uncovered micro-cap); and further product-line tuck-ins as primes keep divesting.

## Risks (be honest with yourself)

- **Micro-cap liquidity** (~280K avg daily shares, ~17M share float) — use limit orders; it can gap on low volume.
- **Lumpy revenue** — military and OEM orders are chunky; any single quarter can miss.
- **Integration/amortization drag** — acquisitions raise D&A and execution risk; watch that margins hold as new lines fold in.
- **Founder control cuts both ways** — strong alignment, but concentrated voting power and limited governance checks.
- **Already volatile** — an $8 → $31 → $18 round-trip in under a year. Expect a bumpy ride; consider scaling in.
- **No analyst coverage** means less institutional support on the way down (and more upside when it arrives).

## Bottom line

**IA is the textbook "overlooked-zone" pick:** a profitable, low-debt, founder-owned specialized-industrial (aerospace avionics) micro-cap that Wall Street literally does not cover, using lean automation to consolidate profitable product lines the giants are throwing away — with revenue that just doubled and a fresh, un-priced Honeywell acquisition set to reveal itself at the December FY26 report.
