## 1. The Ticker & Business Overview — RLGT

**What they do:** Radiant is an **asset-light, technology-enabled third-party logistics (3PL)** company — global freight forwarding, customs brokerage, and value-added supply-chain services across the U.S., Canada, and now Mexico. "Asset-light" is the whole point: Radiant owns almost no trucks or planes. It orchestrates freight across a network of company-owned and agent-based "stations," and keeps the spread between what it bills shippers and what it pays carriers. Capital stays low; returns on tangible capital stay high.

**Why Wall Street ignores them:**
- **Micro-cap, ~$432M market cap, ~$9.21/share** — below almost every institution's and analyst's radar.
- **Freight is in recession.** After the 2021–22 boom and a brutal multi-year down-cycle, transports are the market's unloved corner while everyone crowds into mega-cap AI. Radiant trades closer to the low end of its multi-year range, not the high.
- **It screens as "low margin."** Headline net margin looks thin (~0.4%) because revenue is mostly pass-through freight cost. Screeners miss that the business runs on **net revenue (adjusted gross profit)**, where economics are far better.
- **Lumpy, acquisition-built story** that doesn't fit a clean screen.

## 2. The Financial Checkup

*(FY ends June 30; FY2026 just reported.)*

- **Profits & growth:** FY2026 revenue **~$934M (+3.5%)**, diluted **EPS ~$0.39 (+11%)**. The cycle is turning: **Q4 FY2026 revenue +18.5%** YoY and **adjusted EBITDA +31.6%** — the inflection is already showing up.
- **Cash generation:** FY2026 **adjusted EBITDA ~$36.7M = ~14.9% of adjusted gross profit.** Asset-light means high free-cash conversion; management is buying back stock (585,050 shares / ~$3.5M in nine months).
- **Debt health:** **No net debt.** They *extended* (not drew down) a $200M revolver out to 2031 — i.e., dry powder for tuck-in M&A, not a balance-sheet hole. This is a self-funding model.
- **Valuation:** ~24x trailing earnings, ~1.8x book — not dirt-cheap on GAAP EPS (which carries amortization drag), but inexpensive on EBITDA/FCF for a debt-free, founder-run compounder at a cycle trough.

**Honest caveat:** FY2026 EPS was flattered by a ~$6.2M non-cash earn-out revaluation and a ~$4.0M amortization roll-off. Underlying earnings power is the story, not the reported number.

## 3. The Competitive Moat

- **Founder alignment:** Founder/CEO **Bohn Crain owns ~21.8%** of the company. He built it from scratch starting in 2001 and has skin in the game most micro-caps lack — exactly the insider alignment you asked for.
- **Asset-light network + switching costs:** Radiant's edge isn't trucks; it's the **agent-station network and the operating/technology platform** that ties independent logistics operators into one system. Once a shipper's workflow (customs, tracking, documents, multi-modal routing) lives on Radiant's platform, ripping it out is painful.
- **Tech/automation as the margin lever (your condition #3 — *using* tech, not building AI to sell):** Radiant runs the **Navegate** supply-chain visibility/management platform and has deployed **"Ray," its first AI agent**, to automate the labor-intensive back office (quoting, track-and-trace, documentation, exception handling). In a people-heavy industry, automating these tasks is how an asset-light operator **expands net-revenue margin without adding headcount** — scaling profits faster than peers who are still doing it by hand.
- Big asset-based carriers (the FedEx/UPS tier) can't easily replicate a boutique, tech-enabled, mid-market forwarding network without cannibalizing their own asset base.

## 4. The Specific Growth Trigger

Three catalysts stacking into the next 2–4 quarters:

1. **Freight-cycle upturn (the big one).** Transports are coming off a historic down-cycle. Radiant's **Q4 FY2026 already inflected** (EBITDA +31.6%). As volumes and spreads normalize off a low base, an asset-light operator sees **outsized operating leverage** — profits recover far faster than revenue.
2. **Nearshoring / reshoring of supply chains.** Radiant bought **80% of Mexico-based Weport**, planting a flag in the single biggest structural freight tailwind of the decade: production and sourcing moving from Asia to **Mexico and North America**, which multiplies cross-border and domestic freight lanes — exactly the macro you want exposure to.
3. **Automation-driven margin expansion.** As Navegate + the "Ray" AI agent scale, net-revenue margins should step up — a company-specific catalyst independent of the cycle.

**Near-term dates to watch:** fiscal Q1 2027 earnings (~early November 2026) and the **annual meeting on November 16, 2026**.

## Risks (so this is honest)

- **Cyclical.** If the freight recession deepens or industrial demand stalls, the recovery thesis slips.
- **Thin absolute margins** inherent to forwarding — this is a spread/returns-on-capital story, not a high-gross-margin software story.
- **Acquisition-dependent growth** and purchase-accounting noise in reported EPS.
- **Micro-cap illiquidity** (~150k–390k shares/day) — position sizing and limit orders matter.
- **Agent/station concentration** risk within the network.

The only reason it's the runner-up: it already **tripled in 2026** and trades ~44x trailing, so you'd be buying quality *after* Wall Street started paying attention — the opposite of the "overlooked-zone entry" you asked for.
