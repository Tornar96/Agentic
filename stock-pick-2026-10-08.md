## 1. The Ticker & Business Overview

- **Ticker:** **IA** (Nasdaq). It traded as **ISSC** until the ticker switched to IA on **Aug 18, 2026**, alongside a rebrand from "Innovative Solutions & Support" to **Innovative Aerosystems**. Headquartered in Exton, PA; ~147 employees.
- **What they do:** They make and *certify* avionics — flight displays, air-data computers, autothrottle/flight-guidance systems, inertial/navigation and communication units — mainly for **retrofitting existing aircraft** (commercial, business, and **military**) rather than chasing new-plane OEM slots. Think of them as the shop that modernizes the "glass cockpit" and critical flight systems on the enormous installed base of aging airframes.
- **Why Wall Street ignores them:** (a) **Too small** — a $342M micro-cap with one analyst's worth of coverage; most institutions have a liquidity/size floor it falls under. (b) **Unglamorous niche** — "legacy avionics retrofit" is the boring, back-of-the-aircraft corner of aerospace, not the sexy eVTOL/defense-prime headline space. (c) **Lumpy optics** — quarterly revenue swings on program timing (e.g., F-16 deliveries), which masks the underlying ~40% organic growth and scares off momentum traders.

**Valuation:** P/E **~18** on a business compounding revenue double-digits-to-40% with 28% ROE — reasonable, not a bubble.

## 3. The Competitive Moat

- **Certification is the wall.** Every avionics box needs FAA **TSO/STC** (and military equivalents) approvals that take years and deep money to earn. Once IA's hardware is **designed into a certified aircraft configuration**, swapping it out means re-certifying — so the installed base becomes a long, sticky, aftermarket-and-spares annuity. A larger competitor cannot simply undercut on price.
- **The capital-light "buy-and-build" edge (the tech/efficiency angle).** IA isn't an AI company — its advantage is *using* engineering and modern software-defined avionics to **absorb product lines the primes consider too small to bother with** (Honeywell's inertial/navigation/communication and military display lines in 2023–24; Aydin Displays in 2026), then run them at far higher margins on a lean 147-person base (~$650K revenue/employee). It digitizes and consolidates legacy analog systems that the giants are divesting — a structural **margin-expansion machine** rather than a one-product bet.
- **Insider alignment:** insiders own **~36%**. Note honestly: founder **Geoffrey Hedrick died in Jan 2022**; that large stake now sits with his estate/family plus long-tenured management (CEO Shahram Askarpour), so interests remain tightly tied to the share price — but this is no longer a living-founder story.

## 4. The Specific Growth Trigger

**Primary catalyst — the product-line roll-up compounding into the model.** The **Aydin Displays acquisition (closed July 2026, ~$16M of added annual revenue)** stacks on top of the Honeywell lines, and each deal is margin-accretive and cross-sellable into IA's certified customer base. As these integrate, revenue steps toward the **$250M long-term target** (from a ~$99M FY26 run-rate) — the clearest, most concrete value driver.

**Supporting tailwinds you're paying little for:**
- **Defense modernization + reshoring of avionics supply chains:** as primes shed non-core lines and the DoD pushes retrofit/upgrade of existing fleets, IA is a direct, domestic beneficiary (military displays, flight-control computers).
- **Aging global fleet:** airlines and operators are upgrading cockpits on aircraft they're keeping longer — pure retrofit demand, IA's core.
- **Re-rating optic:** the Aug 2026 ticker/brand change to "IA / Innovative Aerosystems" plus the $250M roadmap is exactly the kind of visibility event that pulls a sub-$350M micro-cap onto more screens.

**Watch items / risks:** quarterly lumpiness (program timing, e.g., F-16); integration and leverage from serial M&A; thin trading liquidity; and the post-founder governance question. Size the position accordingly.
