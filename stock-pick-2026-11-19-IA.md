## 1. The Ticker & Business Overview — *what they do, why Wall Street ignores them*

IA designs and builds **cockpit avionics** — air-data computers, engine/flight displays, autothrottle and flight-guidance systems, standby instruments — for both **retrofit** (upgrading existing aircraft) and **OEM** programs. Customers span commercial air transport, business jets, and increasingly **military/defense** platforms (e.g., autothrottle and display retrofits on legacy fleets that still need to fly for decades).

The real engine isn't organic widgets — it's a **"buy-the-orphan-product-line" roll-up**: IA acquires mature, already-FAA-certified avionics product lines that primes no longer want to bother with (recent deals: **Honeywell** autopilot & generator lines, **S-TEC**, and legacy **Aydin/Sparton** display lines — ~$33.5M of acquisitions), then runs them on its tiny 147-person footprint at far higher margin than the sellers ever did. Revenue went from **$27.7M (FY22) → $47.2M (FY24) → $84.3M (FY25)**, roughly tripling in three years.

**Why Wall Street ignores it:** $336M cap, essentially no sell-side coverage, ~227K shares/day of volume, a boring sub-sector, and a confusing ticker/name change mid-2026. It's too small for funds to own and too dull for retail. That's the overlooked zone.

## 3. The Competitive Moat — *why bigger players don't just take the niche*

- **FAA certification wall.** Each product carries hard-won Technical Standard Orders and Supplemental Type Certificates (STCs). Re-certifying an equivalent box costs years and millions — a deterrent that protects a small installed base a prime would never chase.
- **Installed-base aftermarket.** Avionics on a platform get serviced/spared for the 20–30-year life of the airframe. Once IA owns the line, it owns a captive, high-margin annuity.
- **The acquirer's edge is structural.** Primes like Honeywell *shed* these lines precisely because they're too small to matter to a $100B company — but they're transformative for a $336M one. IA's lean cost base is the moat: it can profitably run what the seller couldn't.
- **Tech/automation advantage (criterion #3).** This is the softest leg, so I'll be straight: IA isn't an "AI company." Its edge is engineering IP + a highly automated, 147-person manufacturing footprint that lets it absorb acquired OEM product lines and *expand gross margin from ~36% to ~52%* — margin expansion through operational/engineering efficiency ahead of peers, which is exactly what the brief asks for, even if it's not a buzzword.

## 4. The Specific Growth Trigger — *the catalyst*

1. **Acquired-line ramp (the big one).** Per the latest 10-Q, the Honeywell/Aydin purchase accounting is still **provisional** — specified inventory, equipment, and IP had *not yet fully transferred* at quarter-end. Translation: a meaningful slug of acquired revenue hasn't hit the P&L yet. As those lines fully transfer and ramp through FY2027, revenue steps up mechanically.
2. **Backlog conversion.** Backlog ~$83–87M (roughly a full year of sales), with management guiding **~78% to be recognized within 12 months** — high visibility for a micro-cap, and it excludes potential sole-source production orders from development programs (upside).
3. **Imminent print.** Fiscal year ends Sept 30; FY2025 was reported in early December. **The FY2026 full-year report is due in the next few weeks** — the first clean look at the full acquired-line base and a near-term catalyst/risk event.

## Why not the "obvious" small-cap — PSIX (Power Solutions International)
PSIX has the louder tailwind (data-center backup power, +52% FY25 revenue) and a cheap ~11x P/E, and it passed my quality screen. I rejected it for *this* brief because it fails the two criteria that define the strategy:
- **Not overlooked:** it spiked to $106 in Feb 2026 and **crashed ~28% in a single day in March 2026**; it's a well-known, heavily-traded momentum name — the opposite of pre-discovery.
- **Insider alignment is wrong:** it's **controlled by China's Weichai Power** (~60%+), not aligned founders, and now faces **multiple securities class-action lawsuits** alleging it misled investors about data-center capacity. Governance + litigation overhang conflict directly with "founders aligned with shareholders."

## Key risks on IA (own them before you buy)
- **Acquisition-dependent growth** — the story breaks if the deal pipeline dries up or integration slips; provisional purchase accounting adds estimate risk.
- **Rising debt** from M&A; watch leverage and interest.
- **Thin liquidity & concentration** — ~227K shares/day; a few customers/programs drive results; the **founder's estate has been a share seller**, and insider-ownership figures vary by source (~25% aggregate, but trending down as the estate distributes).
- **Lumpy quarters** — defense/retrofit orders are timing-driven; margins bounced from 10% to 32% across recent quarters.
- **Valuation already re-rated** — up from an ~$8 low a year ago to ~$19; it's no longer dirt cheap at ~18x earnings, so entry timing matters.
