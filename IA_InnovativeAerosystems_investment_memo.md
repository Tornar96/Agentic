# The Overlooked Pick: Innovative Aerosystems (NASDAQ: IA)

**Prepared:** November 23, 2026 · **Price at writing:** $18.33 · **Market cap:** ~$328M
*Formerly Innovative Solutions & Support (ticker ISSC until Aug 18, 2026).*

> One-line thesis: A founder-led, ~$330M micro-cap that quietly rolls up *orphaned, FAA-certified avionics product lines* the big primes no longer want — running them at 50%+ gross margins with 147 people and **zero sell-side analyst coverage**. It sits dead-center in the aerospace/defense re-shoring and fleet-retrofit cycle, and the market has it priced ~40% below its spring high.

---

## 1. The Ticker & Business Overview

**What they do.** IA designs, certifies, manufactures and services cockpit avionics — flat-panel flight displays, flight management systems, autothrottles, air-data computers, standby instruments and autopilots — mostly for the **retrofit/aftermarket** of existing commercial, military and business aircraft, plus some OEM. Think of them as the company that re-equips the "glass cockpit" on aging 737s, military transports (e.g., KC-46/legacy tankers), Pilatus PC-12s and now eVTOLs. Founded 1988 by **Geoffrey Hedrick** (still Executive Chairman); CEO Shahram Askarpour; HQ Exton, PA; **only 147 employees**.

**The roll-up engine.** The real model, accelerated in 2025–26, is acquiring *small, legacy product lines that are immaterial to the primes but have captive, certified installed bases*. In early 2026 alone they bought **Honeywell's Autopilot line, Honeywell Generators, S-TEC, and Sparton Aydin**. These come with existing FAA/EASA certifications and recurring spares demand — then IA runs them far leaner than a $30B prime ever could.

**Why Wall Street ignores them.**
- **No analyst coverage at all** — not a single sell-side rating in the data. It is structurally off-screen.
- **Tiny and illiquid** — ~$328M cap, ~17M-share float, boring "regulated avionics" story with none of the AI-hype shine.
- **A confusing year** — rebranded from Innovative Solutions & Support to *Innovative Aerosystems* (Oct 2025) and changed its ticker from **ISSC → IA** on Aug 18, 2026. Name/ticker churn tends to orphan a stock in screens and databases.
- **Beaten down** — trades at $18.33 vs. a 52-week high of $30.94 (Mar 2026); it bottomed at $8.44 a year ago. The re-rating hasn't been noticed.

## 3. The Competitive Moat

**Certification is the wall.** Every avionics box is tied to an FAA/EASA **Supplemental Type Certificate (STC)** for a specific aircraft type. Earning one is slow, expensive and regulated; ripping out a certified box to switch vendors means re-certifying the whole installation. That makes IA's installed base extraordinarily sticky and creates a decades-long **aftermarket/spares annuity** on hardware already flying.

**The "orphaned product line" niche.** IA's edge is buying certified lines the primes consider too small to bother with. A $30B prime can't profitably manage a $10M niche product; IA can run it at 50% gross margin. Larger competitors *don't want* this share — it's beneath their cost structure — which is precisely why the niche is defensible.

**Tech used to widen margins (not an "AI builder").** This is the criterion that separates IA from a commodity manufacturer: **147 employees generate ~$93M of revenue (~$630K/employee)**. They achieve that through vertically-integrated, software-defined avionics (their own flight-management and display software IP) and automated design/test/integration workflows that collapse certification and production cost. The proof is in the tape — gross margin went from ~35% to >50% in four quarters even while absorbing four acquisitions. They *use* engineering and automation to out-margin peers, exactly the "efficient tech adopter" profile.

## 4. The Specific Growth Trigger

Several catalysts stack in the next 1–4 quarters:

1. **FY2026 full-year earnings — tentatively Dec 17, 2026** (consensus EPS ~$0.27 for Q4). The first clean quarter showing all four 2026 acquisitions contributing — management guided three of them to **~$10M incremental annual revenue at ~50% gross margin**. A beat here is the nearest hard catalyst (~3 weeks out).
2. **The $50M eVTOL contract** disclosed alongside Q3 — a large, multi-year award relative to a ~$93M revenue base, and a foothold in the fastest-growing new-aircraft category.
3. **Defense revenue ramp** — management explicitly flags an anticipated defense inflection (cockpit modernization/displays), riding the broader re-shoring and defense-modernization spend cycle.
4. **The coverage/re-rating gap** — with *zero* analysts following it, a freshly rebranded, profitable, 50%-gross-margin compounder is one initiation report or one index/ETF inclusion away from a step-change in sponsorship. Backlog (~$83M) and a "very active" acquisition pipeline keep the flywheel turning.

## Alignment & the honest risks

- **Insider alignment:** insiders own roughly a third of the company (~36% per Robinhood data), anchored by founder/Chairman Hedrick — true owner-operator skin in the game.
- **Risks to weigh:** (1) leverage tripled for M&A — integration must deliver; (2) micro-cap illiquidity and a thin ~17M-share float mean sharp volatility; (3) program/customer concentration and a recent dip in legacy *military* revenue (part of why they're acquiring); (4) backlog isn't a straight line (it slipped from $87M in Mar to $83M in Jun); (5) key-man risk around the founder.

**Bottom line:** IA checks every box in the mandate — sub-$2B (far under, at ~$330M), genuinely high-quality (50% gross margin, ~28% ROE, self-funding, modest leverage), heavy insider ownership, a deeply unloved/uncovered corner of aerospace, a real re-shoring/defense/retrofit tailwind, and a clear tech-driven margin-expansion story — with a hard catalyst roughly three weeks out.
